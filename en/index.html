MoR Ops privacy policy

Last updated: 23 November 2026 (policy version 1.8: morops.app website)

Español · Italiano · English

MoR Ops is an emergency preparedness and management app for Android (identifier com.mor.morops). This policy explains what data the app uses, where it is stored, what it is used for and what control you have over it, under the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

In short
  • To use the app you create an account with your email address and a password. The account server stores only your email, your name or nickname, the app language and your encrypted password, in Germany (European Union), plus an access log that is deleted after 30 days.
  • Everything else you enter (your group, health data, grab bag, supplies, maps and saved points) stays on your phone only.
  • We use no analytics, no advertising and no third-party SDKs that track your activity. We do not sell or share your data.
  • The app only connects to the internet for your account, OpenStreetMap maps and alerts. It never sends your exact location.
  • You can delete your account from the app at any time.
  • The morops.app website uses no cookies, analytics or third-party services, and stores nothing in your browser unless you ask it to (section 17).

1. Data controller

The data controller is Nicolas Mansilla Benedetti, developer of MoR Ops. Email: nicomor3@gmail.com. For any question about your data, use the contact details in section 16.

2. Your account

  • What the server stores: your email address; your password, only in encrypted form (a bcrypt hash that nobody can read); the name or nickname you choose; the app language, so that we write to you in your language; and the dates of sign-up, confirmation and last sign-in.
  • What for: to create and protect your account, confirm your email address, reset your password and warn you if someone changes it. Legal basis: performance of a contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR); without this data we cannot give you an account.
  • Emails: only the necessary ones: the code to confirm your email, the code to reset your password and the notice that your password was changed. Never advertising.
  • Sessions: the app does not keep a session open on the server. It connects only to create the account, sign in, change the password or delete the account, and closes the session when done. If it cannot close it (for example, because the signal dropped), the server deletes it within 2 days, together with the IP address and device type it stores.
  • Access log: for security and to detect abuse (for example, mass sign-in attempts), the server logs the date, the type of action (creating the account, signing in, changing the password), the IP address, your email address and your account ID. The server's error logs also contain the IP address. Both are deleted after 30 days. Legal basis: legitimate interest in the security of the service (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).
  • How long: for as long as you have the account. If you create an account and do not confirm your email, it is deleted automatically after 7 days.

4. Data that stays on your phone only

The information you enter in the app is stored in a local database, in the app's private storage on your device, and is not uploaded to any server. It may include:

  • Members of your group: name, role, blood type, allergies, skills, health status, notes, last seen location and recorded medication doses.
  • Supply inventory, tasks, recorded crises, threat assessments and notes.
  • Your 72-hour grab bag list: what you ticked off, the expiry dates you noted and when you last checked it.
  • Emergency contacts you add manually.
  • Points saved on the map (waypoints) and their location.

This data never leaves your phone unless you decide to share it (sections 8 and 9). The app has no access to your contacts, photos or files, except the single file you choose when restoring a backup or importing an offline map.

5. Location

With your permission, the app uses the device location only while it is open, to:

  • show your position on the map;
  • include your coordinates in an SOS message, the "Tell contacts you're OK" message or a help report when you send it;
  • save a point on the map with your current location.

If you turn on alerts (section 7), the app stores on the phone the last location obtained while the app was open, rounded to about 1 km, to know whether an alert affects you even when the app is closed. It is replaced with every new location and ignored after a week.

The app does not access GPS in the background, does not keep a history of your movements and does not send your location to any server (not even the account server). You can refuse or revoke the permission at any time in Android's settings; the rest of the app keeps working.

6. Maps (OpenStreetMap)

To show the map, the app downloads images (tiles) from the OpenStreetMap Foundation's servers (tile.openstreetmap.org). As with any web connection, that service receives your IP address, the map area requested and an identifier of the app. The app sends no other data. Tiles are cached on the phone so they can be used offline. OpenStreetMap's processing of this data is governed by its privacy policy.

7. Alerts and events

The alerts feature shows on the map, and notifies you of, weather warnings, earthquakes, tsunamis, major disasters, fires detected by satellite, the travel risk level by country and, only if you turn that layer on, automatically detected (unverified) news of violence.

  • What is downloaded and from where: public files from the United States Geological Survey (USGS), the European-Mediterranean Seismological Centre (EMSC), GDACS (European Commission and UN), national weather services (or the Filtered Alert Hub aggregator), MeteoAlarm (EUMETNET) for weather warnings in Italy, NOAA's tsunami warning centres, NASA FIRMS, the foreign ministries of the United Kingdom (FCDO) and the United States, and The GDELT Project (only with the news layer turned on).
  • What those services receive: your IP address and a general request (a worldwide, country or continent file). For EMSC earthquakes, and for fires if you enter a personal NASA FIRMS key, the request specifies a large rectangular region (at least about 500 km wide, aligned to a grid) containing your areas. Your exact location, your areas and their names are never sent.
  • Weather warnings in Italy: the app downloads MeteoAlarm's public feed for Italy and the CAP file of each warning. MeteoAlarm receives only your IP address and a general request for the whole country, never your location. The boundaries of the Italian regions, from ISTAT, are built into the app and do not generate any request.
  • Where the matching happens: the phone decides whether an event is near your areas or your last location.
  • Notifications: they are generated by the phone itself; there is no server sending you alerts. To check the sources while the app is closed, Android's task scheduler (WorkManager) is used.
  • Watched areas and settings: stored on the phone only, included in the backups you create and deleted with "Delete all data".

Alerts are for information and may arrive late: MoR Ops is not an official early warning system. Keep your phone's emergency alerts turned on (for example, IT-alert in Italy or EU-Alert in other EU countries).

8. SOS messages, calls and reports

When you send an SOS or use "Tell contacts you're OK", call an emergency number or share a help report, the app opens your messaging app, your phone app or your system's share menu with the text ready. You choose the recipient and confirm the sending; MoR Ops never sends messages on its own and receives no copy of them.

9. Backups

Only when you ask for it, the app creates a backup file with your data and opens the share menu so you can decide where to save it (for example, Drive or a local folder). You can protect it with a password: in that case it is encrypted with AES-256-GCM and cannot be read without that password. Android's automatic backups are turned off for this app, so your data is not uploaded to the cloud unless you decide so.

10. Security

  • You sign in with your email address and your password. The password travels encrypted (HTTPS) and the server stores only a hash of it.
  • To unlock the app offline, the phone keeps a value derived from your password (PBKDF2) in the system's secure storage, never the password itself.
  • If you turn it on, you can unlock with the fingerprint or face registered on the phone. Android does the check: the app only receives whether it succeeded, and never accesses or stores biometric data. Your password keeps working.
  • The app locks itself after a few minutes in the background and temporarily blocks access after several failed attempts. The server also limits attempts.

12. How to delete your data

  • Your account: in the app, go to More → Settings → under "Account and security", tap your email address → Delete account. The account (email, name, language and password) is deleted from the server immediately, except for the access log (with your email address and IP address), which is kept for up to 30 days. The server's backups may keep this data for up to 14 more days, and the server's error logs for up to 30 days from when they are created; they are then deleted automatically. You can also ask for deletion by email, without having the app: the steps, what is deleted and what is kept are on the Delete your MoR Ops account page.
  • The data on your phone: in More → Settings → Delete all data, or by uninstalling the app.
  • Backups you exported: they stay where you saved them and you need to delete them yourself.

13. Your rights

You have the right to access your data, rectify it, erase it, restrict or object to its processing, and receive it in a portable format. You can exercise several of them directly in the app (changing your password, deleting your account); for the rest, write to the contact in section 16 and we will reply within one month.

If you believe your data is not being handled properly, you can lodge a complaint with the data protection authority in your country: in Italy, the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali; in Spain, the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos; elsewhere, the competent authority.

14. Children

The app is designed for adults who organise the preparedness of their household or group. It is not directed at children under 16 and does not knowingly collect data about them; data about underage members entered by an adult stays only on that adult's device.

15. Important notice and changes

The information in the app is for guidance only and does not replace professional medical care or the instructions of emergency services or the authorities. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (112 anywhere in the European Union).

If this policy changes, it will be published at this same address with the date of the update. If a change means using data in a new way, the app will tell you beforehand.

16. Contact

For privacy questions or to exercise your rights, write to nicomor3@gmail.com (the same contact email shown on the MoR Ops listing on Google Play). You can also ask general questions by opening an issue at github.com/MansillaNicolas/mor-ops-privacidad/issues, but GitHub issues are public: they are not the place to ask for your account to be deleted or to send personal data. Use the contact email for that.

17. The morops.app website

The morops.app website is static: it has no accounts or forms, uses no cookies, analytics or advertising, and loads no third-party fonts, scripts, maps or videos.

  • Who serves it: the same server as the MoR Ops API, hosted by Hetzner Online GmbH in Nuremberg (Germany, European Union), which is listed as a processor in section 3. The server receives your IP address and the technical details of the request only to answer it, and keeps no log of each visit. If there is a technical error, the server's error log may contain the IP address; it is deleted after 30 days. Legal basis: the legitimate interest in the security of the service (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).
  • DNS: the domain's DNS is at Cloudflare, without proxy: Cloudflare does not receive visits to the site.
  • In your browser, only if you ask for it: the copy for using the site offline (when you tap "Save for offline use") and the backpack list (when you tick something on it or change the number of people). They stay on your device, are not sent to the server, and are deleted with the "Delete what is saved in this browser" button or from your browser settings. A normal visit saves nothing.
  • Third-party links: the guides and the list of sources link to the sites of official bodies and organisations (for example civil protection authorities, the Red Cross or health agencies), to this policy (GitHub Pages) and, once it is available, to Google Play. These are all third-party sites, with their own privacy policy. The site sends the Referrer-Policy: no-referrer header, so it does not tell them which page you came from.
  • Changes: if any of this changes, this policy is updated first.
